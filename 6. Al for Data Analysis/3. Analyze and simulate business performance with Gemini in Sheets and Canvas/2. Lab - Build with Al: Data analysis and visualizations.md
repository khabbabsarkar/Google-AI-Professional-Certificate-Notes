## 1. Introduction

Data analysis helps us understand information and make better decisions.

However, analyzing large spreadsheets can take time, especially when we need to calculate metrics, find trends, explain results, and create charts.

Gemini in Google Sheets can make this process easier.

In this lab, you will use Gemini to:
- Get a summary of spreadsheet data.
- Ask questions about your data.
- Understand why certain trends may happen.
- Create charts and visualizations.
- Turn data into useful reports and presentations.

## 2. Lab Requirements

Before starting, make sure you:

- Have a Google Account.
- Can access the Gemini app.
- Can access Google Sheets.
- Are signed in to Gemini and Google Sheets.

You can use your own dataset or the sample CSV file provided in the lab.

## 3. What You Will Learn

The lab has four main steps:

1. Get a high-level data summary.
2. Ask questions about your data.
3. Use AI to explain data findings.
4. Create data visualizations.

## 4. Step 1: Get a High-Level Data Summary

First, import your data into a new Google Sheet.

Then:

1. Open your spreadsheet.
2. Click the Gemini icon in the top-right corner.
3. Open the Gemini sidebar.
4. Enter a prompt to summarize your data.

### Example Prompt

Imagine you have an e-commerce dataset.

```text
This is data from my e-commerce website.

Provide the following metrics:

1. Total purchase revenue.
2. Total number of transactions.
3. Average purchase value.
4. Total number of customers.

Present the results in a clear table.

Explain each metric in simple English.
```

Review Gemini's summary.

You can also click **Analysis steps** to see the code Gemini generated for its analysis.

This helps you understand how Gemini worked with your data.

## 5. Step 2: Ask a Question About Your Data

After getting the summary, ask a more specific question.

The goal is to discover useful information that may not be obvious from the basic metrics.

### Example Question

```text
What is the average revenue per session
based on marketing channel,
regardless of device category?

Show the results in a table
and identify the highest-performing channel.
```

Gemini can help compare the results across different channels.

For example:

| Marketing Channel | Average Revenue per Session |
|---|---:|
| Organic Search | 12.50 |
| Paid Search | 18.75 |
| Social Media | 9.25 |
| Email | 21.00 |

*These are example values, not actual lab results.*

Review the output and select one important finding.

## 6. Step 3: Use Your Data to Build a Narrative

Numbers show what happened, but they do not always explain why it happened.

Gemini can help explore possible reasons behind a finding.

Open a new browser tab and visit:

[Gemini](https://gemini.google.com)

Use the following prompt.

### Example Prompt

```text
You are an experienced data analyst.

For an e-commerce website,
why might email marketing have
a higher average revenue per session
than social media?

Consider possible reasons such as:
- Customer interest.
- Purchase intention.
- Marketing messages.
- Audience behavior.
- Customer engagement.

Separate possible explanations
from facts supported by the data.

Do not present assumptions as proven facts.
```

Gemini may suggest possible explanations, such as:

- Email users may already know the brand.
- Email campaigns may reach interested customers.
- Social media visitors may be browsing rather than buying.
- Different channels may attract different types of customers.

**Important:** These are possible explanations, not proven causes. You need more evidence to confirm why a trend happened.

## 7. Step 4: Create a Data Visualization

Now return to your Google Sheets tab.

Open the Gemini sidebar and ask it to create a chart based on your selected metric.

### Example Prompt

```text
Create a bar chart that shows
average revenue per session
by marketing channel.

Use:
- Marketing channel on the horizontal axis.
- Average revenue per session on the vertical axis.
- A clear chart title.
- Easy-to-read labels.

Add the chart to this spreadsheet.
```

Gemini will generate the chart and add it to your sheet.

Review the chart to make sure it represents the correct data.

### Common Chart Types

| Chart Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Bar Chart | Compare different categories. |
| Line Chart | Show changes over time. |
| Pie Chart | Show parts of a whole. |
| Column Chart | Compare values across groups. |
| Scatter Plot | Explore relationships between two variables. |

## 8. Cybersecurity Applications

You can apply the same workflow to cybersecurity datasets.

### Example 1: SOC Performance Analysis

Imagine you have a spreadsheet containing monthly SOC data.

**Step 1: Get a summary**

```text
This is data from a Security Operations Center.

Provide:
- Total security alerts.
- Total confirmed incidents.
- Average detection time.
- Average response time.

Present the results in a table.
```

**Step 2: Ask a question**

```text
Which month had the highest number
of confirmed security incidents?

Compare the monthly results
and identify important changes.
```

**Step 3: Build a narrative**

```text
You are a cybersecurity data analyst.

Why might the number of confirmed
security incidents have increased
during this month?

Suggest possible explanations.

Separate data-supported findings
from assumptions.
```

**Step 4: Create a visualization**

```text
Create a line chart showing
the number of confirmed security incidents
by month.

Use clear labels and a suitable title.
```

### Example 2: Vulnerability Management

You can also analyze vulnerability records.

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Total Vulnerabilities | Understand the overall workload. |
| Critical Vulnerabilities | Identify the most serious findings. |
| Resolved Vulnerabilities | Track completed remediation. |
| Unresolved Vulnerabilities | Identify remaining work. |
| Remediation Rate | Measure remediation progress. |

Example prompt:

```text
Analyze this vulnerability dataset.

Show the number of vulnerabilities
by severity level.

Compare resolved and unresolved issues.

Create a bar chart showing
vulnerabilities by severity.
```

## 10. Responsible AI Practices

Always use AI carefully when working with data.

- Do not upload passwords or private information.
- Avoid sharing confidential company data.
- Use approved tools for sensitive datasets.
- Check calculations against the original data.
- Verify that charts use the correct metrics.
- Do not treat possible explanations as proven facts.
- Review AI-generated code and analysis steps when possible.

For cybersecurity work, use sanitized or approved datasets whenever possible.

## 11. Key Takeaways

- Gemini can summarize spreadsheet data.
- Natural language questions make data analysis easier.
- AI can help identify trends and important findings.
- Gemini can suggest possible explanations for data patterns.
- Charts help communicate complex information.
- Data summaries, narratives, and visualizations work together.
- Human review is necessary before making decisions.

Good data analysis is not just about calculating numbers. It is about understanding what those numbers mean, explaining why they matter, and using them to make better decisions.
