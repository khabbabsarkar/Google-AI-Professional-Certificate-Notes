## Introduction

AI can help us with many tasks, but it is not always correct or fair.

As a responsible AI user, you should understand its limitations. Some common limitations are:

1. Bias
2. Knowledge Cutoff
3. Factual Drift
4. Behavioral Drift

Understanding these limitations helps us use AI more carefully and responsibly.

## 1. Understand Bias in AI

AI bias happens when an AI model produces unfair or unbalanced results.

This can happen when its training data is:

- Incomplete
- Incorrect
- Unbalanced
- Based on historical or social bias

AI learns patterns from its training data. If the data contains bias, AI may repeat that bias in its answers.

### Example

Imagine asking AI to generate an image of an office.

It may repeatedly show a modern office in a high-rise building. However, offices can also be small rooms, home offices, or creative workspaces.

This happens because AI may have learned certain patterns from its training data.

### How to Reduce AI Bias

**1. Give clear instructions**

Explain what type of result you want. Include details about your audience and needs.

**2. Provide balanced references**

Give AI different examples so it does not focus on only one type of information.

**3. Correct biased answers**

If AI gives a biased answer, explain the problem and ask it to provide a fairer response.

**Example Prompt:**

> Review your previous answer. It may contain an unfair stereotype. Identify the problem and provide a more balanced and fair response.

## 2. Understand Knowledge Cutoff

A knowledge cutoff is the date when the training information of an AI model ends.

This means the model may not know about:

- Recent security threats
- New technology
- Recent discoveries
- New software updates
- Current news and events

Some AI tools can search the internet for current information. However, this is different from the model's original training knowledge.

### Cybersecurity Example

Imagine asking an AI model:

> What are the latest cybersecurity threats in 2026?

If the model does not have access to current information, it may give outdated answers.

It may also mention old vulnerabilities as if they are new.

### How to Handle Knowledge Cutoff

**1. Check the cutoff date**

Find out when the AI model's training knowledge ends, if this information is available.

**2. Verify current information**

Check recent information using reliable sources, official reports, and security websites.

**3. Specify the timeframe**

Instead of asking:

> What are the latest cybersecurity threats?

Ask:

> What are the major cybersecurity threats reported in 2026? Include reliable sources and publication dates.

**4. Use follow-up prompts**

If an answer looks outdated, ask AI to find newer information or explain the latest changes.

## 3. Understand AI Drift

AI drift means that an AI model's accuracy, relevance, or behavior can change over time.

There are two common types:

### A. Factual Drift

Factual drift happens when AI gives less useful or outdated information because the world has changed since its training data was collected.

**Example:**

An AI model may recommend an old version of a security tool without knowing that a newer version is available.

### B. Behavioral Drift

Behavioral drift happens when an AI model's response style or behavior changes after developers update it.

For example, the same prompt may produce different:

- Writing styles
- Response formats
- Tone
- Levels of detail

### How to Manage AI Drift

- Provide accurate and updated information.
- Use clear and specific prompts.
- Start a new chat for a different task.
- Review AI responses regularly.
- Check important facts before using them.

## 4. Cybersecurity Examples of AI Limitations

| AI Limitation | Cybersecurity Example |
|---|---|
| Bias | An AI security tool may incorrectly flag some normal activities as suspicious because of unbalanced training data. |
| Knowledge Cutoff | AI may not know about a newly discovered vulnerability. |
| Factual Drift | AI may recommend outdated security practices. |
| Behavioral Drift | An AI tool may change its report format after a model update. |

These are examples of possible problems. Unusual AI output does not always mean that the model is wrong, but it should be checked.

## 5. Responsible AI Practices

To use AI responsibly:

1. Check important facts before trusting AI answers.
2. Look for bias and unfair assumptions.
3. Verify recent news and statistics.
4. Provide updated context.
5. Use clear instructions.
6. Review AI-generated content before sharing it.
7. Use human judgment for important decisions.

## Key Notes

- AI can produce biased or unfair answers.
- Training data affects AI output.
- A knowledge cutoff means AI may not know recent information.
- Live web search can help find current information, but its results also need verification.
- Factual drift makes information less accurate as the world changes.
- Behavioral drift can change the way AI responds.
- Human review and critical thinking are important.
