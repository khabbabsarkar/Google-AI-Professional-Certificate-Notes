## 1. Introduction

AI can help us with many tasks, but it does not understand the world like humans do.

It learns patterns from data and uses them to generate answers. Sometimes, it can give incorrect information or make up facts.

Using AI responsibly means knowing its limits and keeping human control over its use.

## 2. Limitations of AI

AI has some important limitations.

### 1. Hallucination

Hallucination happens when AI gives false or made-up information as if it were true.

**Example:**

You ask AI about cybersecurity news from today. It may give you old news or information that is not true.

### 2. Lack of Common Sense

AI does not have human experience or real-world understanding.

It generates answers based on patterns learned from data.

**Example:**

AI may suggest a security action without understanding the full situation or its possible risks.

### 3. Incorrect Information

AI can make mistakes because of poor training data, missing information, or unfamiliar topics.

**Remember:** A confident answer does not always mean it is correct.

## 3. Human-in-the-Loop Approach

Human-in-the-loop means a human stays involved in the AI process.

AI can help with tasks, but humans must review the results and make important decisions.

### Why Is It Important?

- Humans can check whether information is correct.
- Humans can find mistakes and unfair results.
- Humans can understand the real-world situation.
- Humans remain responsible for important decisions.

**Example:**

AI detects unusual network activity and creates a security alert. A human security analyst checks the alert before deciding whether it is a real threat.

## 4. The ACT Framework for Responsible AI

The ACT framework has three simple steps:

### A — Ask

Before using AI, ask yourself:

- Is AI the right tool for this task?
- Can AI handle this task safely?
- Does the task involve private or sensitive information?
- Do I need help from a qualified professional?

**Example:** Do not depend only on AI for medical, legal, or financial advice.

### C — Check

Always review AI-generated answers before using them.

Check for:

- Accuracy: Is the information correct?
- Bias: Is the answer unfair to anyone?
- Relevance: Does it match your needs?
- Objectivity: Is the answer balanced?
- Safety: Could using this answer cause harm?

**Example:** Verify cybersecurity advice with trusted documentation and reliable sources.

### T — Tell

Be honest about how you use AI.

Tell people when AI has helped create or improve content, especially when disclosure is required or important.

**Example:** If AI helped you create a report or presentation, be transparent about its use when appropriate.

## 5. Responsible AI in Cybersecurity

### Example: AI for Cybersecurity Threat Detection

**Scenario:**

Imagine a cybersecurity team uses an AI tool to analyze network traffic and find possible security threats.

AI can process large amounts of data and identify unusual patterns. However, its findings still need human review.

### Applying the ACT Framework

**Ask:**
- Is AI suitable for analyzing this network data?
- Does using the tool follow security and privacy policies?
- Is the data safe to share with this AI tool?

**Check:**
- Review AI-generated security alerts.
- Check for false positives and missed threats.
- Verify findings using logs and other security tools.
- Do not assume every unusual activity is an attack.

**Tell:**
- Inform the team when AI is used in threat detection.
- Explain how AI supports the investigation.
- Make it clear that humans review and confirm important findings.

### Why Is Human Review Important?

AI may identify unusual activity, but unusual activity is not always malicious.

A human analyst needs to understand the situation, investigate the evidence, and decide what action to take.

## 6. Quick Summary

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hallucination | AI creates false or made-up information. |
| AI Limitations | AI can make mistakes and lacks human experience. |
| Human-in-the-Loop | Humans stay involved and review AI results. |
| Ask | Decide whether AI is suitable and safe. |
| Check | Review AI results for accuracy, bias, and safety. |
| Tell | Be transparent about AI use. |

## 7. Responsible AI Checklist

Before using AI, ask yourself:

- [ ] Is AI the right tool for this task?
- [ ] Am I protecting private and sensitive data?
- [ ] Have I checked the AI-generated information?
- [ ] Have I looked for mistakes or bias?
- [ ] Have I verified important facts with trusted sources?
- [ ] Am I being transparent about AI use?
- [ ] Is a human making the final important decision?

## Key Takeaways

- AI is helpful, but it is not always correct.
- Hallucinations can lead to false information.
- Humans must stay involved in important tasks.
- The ACT framework helps us use AI responsibly.
- Always ask, check, and tell.
- In cybersecurity, AI can support threat detection, but human judgment is essential.
