## 1. Introduction

AI can be a powerful assistant, but it does not have human experience, real-world knowledge, or common sense.

AI can help us complete tasks, but humans must stay involved and take responsibility for the results.

This is called the **Human-in-the-Loop approach**.

Responsible AI use is not just about following rules. It is about making good decisions, protecting information, and using AI honestly.

## 2. Protect Sensitive Information

Privacy means protecting personal and private information from unauthorized access or use.

Before using an AI tool, understand:
- What information does the tool collect?
- How does it use your data?
- Who may access your information?
- What do its privacy settings and terms say?

Avoid entering confidential or sensitive information into public AI tools.

### Practical Ways to Protect Information

**1. Use Generic Placeholders**

Replace real names, places, and project names with general words.

Example:

Instead of:
```text
Analyze the security report of ABC Company's
customer database.
```

Use:
```text
Analyze this sample security report for a
company's customer database.
```

**2. Focus on the Task**

Write prompts about the work you need to complete instead of including unnecessary personal details.

**3. Share Only Relevant Information**

Do not upload an entire document if AI only needs a small section to complete the task.

**4. Review AI Memory and Privacy Settings**

Review the AI tool's memory, chat history, and privacy settings. Remove or manage stored information when needed.

**5. Check Privacy Policies**

Understand the tool's data practices before accepting its terms or using it for important work.

### Cybersecurity Example

Imagine you want AI to help analyze a security incident.

Do not paste real customer details, passwords, private IP information, or confidential company logs into a public AI tool without proper authorization.

Use sanitized or sample data instead.

## 3. Understand Regulatory and Compliance Boundaries

Some industries have strict rules about how information is handled and how decisions are made.

Examples include:

- **Healthcare:** Medical records and patient information.
- **Finance:** Financial reports and customer information.
- **Legal:** Legal documents and professional advice.
- **Cybersecurity:** Confidential logs, security reports, and personal data.

AI may be able to help with these tasks, but it does not replace qualified professionals.

### Ask Yourself Three Questions

**1. Does This Task Have Regulatory Requirements?**

Some tasks have special rules for handling information.

Check your organization's policies before using AI.

**2. Are There Licensing or Credential Requirements?**

Some work must be completed or checked by licensed professionals.

AI cannot replace their professional judgment.

**3. Who Is Responsible?**

Humans remain responsible for the information they provide and the results they use.

### Best Practice

Consult your organization's:
- Compliance team
- Legal team
- Regulatory experts
- AI usage policies

Do this before using AI for regulated work.

## 4. Build Accountability into Your Process

Accountability means being able to explain how you used AI and how you checked its results.

Keep a record of important steps so other people can understand and review your work.

### Three Ways to Build Accountability

**1. Track Your Sources**

Keep a record of the sources used in your work.

Separate AI-generated content from your own ideas and contributions.

**2. Make Your Process Auditable**

Be ready to explain how you reached a conclusion.

For example:
- Which AI tool did you use?
- What task did you give it?
- Which parts of the result did you use?
- How did you verify the information?

**3. Document Your Verification Steps**

Keep notes about how you checked AI-generated information, especially for important work.

### Cybersecurity Example

Imagine AI helps you prepare a vulnerability analysis report.

You should record:
- The sources and security tools used.
- The AI-generated suggestions.
- The findings you independently verified.
- The changes you made.
- The final human-reviewed conclusions.

This makes the process easier to review and explain.

## 5. The ACT Checklist for Responsible AI

The ACT framework has three parts:

### A — Ask Yourself

Before using AI, ask:

- **Is AI right for this task?** Do not depend on AI alone for medical, legal, financial, or other professional advice.
- **Is this data safe to use?** Avoid sharing sensitive, personal, confidential, or company information with public AI tools.
- **Am I following the rules?** Check your organization's AI policies and use approved tools.

### C — Check Before You Use the Output

Review the AI response in four areas.

**1. Accuracy**

- Verify facts, names, statistics, and quotes.
- Check important information using trusted sources.
- Look for hallucinations and hidden errors.

**2. Bias and Objectivity**

- Check whether the answer is unfair or biased.
- Look for one-sided opinions.
- Make sure different views are considered.

**3. Appropriateness**

- Check whether the tone fits your audience.
- Make sure the content matches your purpose.
- Review whether the language is suitable for the situation.

**4. Originality and Judgment**

- Edit and improve AI-generated content.
- Add your own knowledge and ideas.
- Change your prompt if the result is not useful.
- Use human judgment before accepting the final result.

### T — Tell People When You Use AI

**1. Be Transparent**

Clearly explain when and how AI helped create content, when disclosure is appropriate or required.

**2. Be Compliant**

Follow your organization's rules about AI disclosure and data protection.

Do not share confidential information with public AI tools.

## 6. Practical Cybersecurity Example: Responsible AI Workflow

Imagine you are preparing a report about suspicious network activity.

| ACT Step | What You Should Do |
|---|---|
| Ask | Check whether AI is approved for this task and whether the data is safe to share. |
| Check | Verify AI findings using logs, security tools, and trusted sources. |
| Tell | Disclose AI involvement when required and explain how findings were reviewed. |

**Important:** AI can support security analysis, but a human must review the evidence and make important decisions.

## 7. Quick Summary

| Best Practice | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Data Privacy | Protect personal and confidential information. |
| Generic Placeholders | Hide real names and sensitive details. |
| Data Minimization | Share only the information needed. |
| Compliance | Follow laws and organizational policies. |
| Accountability | Take responsibility for AI-assisted work. |
| Track Sources | Keep records of information and contributions. |
| Verification | Check AI outputs before using them. |
| Transparency | Disclose AI use when appropriate or required. |

## 8. Responsible AI Checklist

Before using AI, review these points:

- [ ] Is AI suitable for this task?
- [ ] Is the information safe to share?
- [ ] Am I using an approved AI tool?
- [ ] Have I removed unnecessary personal details?
- [ ] Have I checked the accuracy of the response?
- [ ] Have I reviewed the answer for bias?
- [ ] Have I added my own judgment?
- [ ] Have I recorded important sources and checks?
- [ ] Have I followed AI disclosure rules?

## Key Notes

- Keep humans involved in AI-assisted work.
- Protect sensitive and confidential information.
- Follow legal and organizational requirements.
- Keep records of important AI-assisted decisions.
- Verify AI-generated information before using it.
- Be transparent about AI use.
- Use the ACT framework: Ask, Check, and Tell.
